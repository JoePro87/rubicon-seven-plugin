# Faction Generation Reference

Vaarn's factions — a **Minor Faction generator** (local organisations) and the **eight Major Factions** (setting-level powers). Source: *Vaults of Vaarn 2e* preview (Crimson Hound), Referee's Toolbox §2.1 "Factions," printed pp. 85–102. Reconstructed 2026-06-04 from the source PDF.

**Using this in content-forge:** at LOCATION scale, roll the Minor Faction Generator for a local organisation with its own leader, assets, and rival. At REGION / campaign scale, use the Major Factions as standing powers — their **Allies / Enemies** lines define the opposition web (e.g. Cacklemaw ↔ everyone; Hegemony ↔ Seekers / Jigsaw Court / Darkling Sun; Jigsaw Court ↔ Darkling Sun as uneasy fellow-travellers). Track party Reputation per the standing table below; spend positive REP as currency.

---

## Factions Overview

Factions are organisations that PCs and NPCs can belong to. They can be trade-related, political, religious, cultural, or purely social in nature, but in all cases faction members are assumed to have common interests and goals that guide all NPC members. PCs have Reputation (REP) with each faction, expressed as a number between -10 and +10, which changes based on how they act toward and treat members of that faction. Positive REP can be spent as currency in exchange for material goods, access to privileged information, recruitment of followers, use of special faction assets, or tutoring in mystical secrets. Some factions are opposed to one another; gaining reputation with one causes the other's reputation to decrease by an equal amount.

| REP | Standing | Effect |
|-----|----------|--------|
| 10+ | Hero | You are a hero to the faction and its members. They will die to assist you if need be. |
| 4 to 9 | Friend | You are a friend to the faction. Members will help you in any way they can. They will never attack you without cause. |
| 1 to 3 | Liked | The faction has a warm opinion of you. When you encounter a member, the reaction roll has ADV. |
| 0 | Neutral | The faction has no opinion of you. Encounters use the standard reaction table. |
| -1 to -3 | Disliked | The faction has a cold opinion of you. When you encounter a member, the reaction roll has DIS. |
| -4 to -9 | Enemy | Faction members are sworn to harm you. They will always attack you, hinder you, lie to you, or otherwise cause you mischief. |
| -10 or lower | Nemesis | You are considered a deadly threat to the faction and will be hunted down by the most committed members. If the faction is not generally in the business of killing, they hire a third party who is. |

**Major Factions** are organisations with members all over Vaarn, whose goals may change the setting forever. **Minor Factions** are organisations with local power, whose goals may be as simple as survival or acquisition of wealth.

## Major Factions of Vaarn

### The New Hegemony
*A proud, expanding True-Kin empire whose Hegemon claims all of Vaarn as sovereign territory — enforced by red-coated Legionaries, privateers, and divide-and-conquer diplomacy.*

The New Hegemony lies to the south of the blue lands of Vaarn and has long extended its reach into the blue ruins. The Hegemon has decreed all of Vaarn a province of His suzerainty, and red-coated Legionaries patrol the lapis badlands, attempting to bring Hegemonic Law to the wandering Faa tribes and the cacogenic hamlets that cluster around scant water-holes. Further afield, crews of Hegemony privateers raid and trade wherever fortune calls them, most little better than bandits who brandish the Hegemon's seal and writ alongside fearful weapons.

Vaarn is considered the most dangerous and least lucrative of all Hegemony postings; commanders and consuls are assigned there as a punishment or because they lack the family connections to secure better posts. Hegemony soldiers are well-trained and equipped with firearms and military cybernetics preserved from the Long Ago, but have struggled to adapt to Vaarn's harsh climate and enormous travel distances. The Hegemony employs divide-and-conquer tactics, favouring one warring faction over another to slowly weaken both — its diplomats and arms dealers are considered as dangerous as its soldiers.

A Vaarn subdued by the Hegemony becomes a bounded land, fenced and observed and catalogued and regulated: all free peoples subjected to Hegemonic Law and taxed to the limit of their endurance, monsters permitted to exist only in menageries or weapons research facilities. Most Hegemony commanders do not believe such a grand taming is possible, but this is what they strive for regardless.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Neutral until provoked; Legionaries react using the standard table at REP 0. Gaining REP requires gifts of Exotica or trade goods, loyalty oaths, delivery of captives, or defending Hegemony holdings.
- **Joining this Faction:** True-Kin PCs can swear an oath to serve the Hegemon and enter the staff of a Hegemony Consul or serve as a Legionary. PCs from other ancestries may find work as scouts, translators, advisors, or irregular soldiers, but will never be considered full citizens or given command over Hegemony troops.
- **Allies:** Faa Nomads (some tribes act as scouts and auxiliary soldiers in exchange for protection of their hunting grounds and holy places; the Hegemony view the alliance as temporary, to be broken as soon as expedient).
- **Enemies:** Cacklemaw Clans (killed Hegemony diplomats; to be killed on sight); Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom (psychic prowess and refusal to negotiate have made them official enemies); Court of the Jigsaw Autarch (its goal of expanding hypergeometric Labyrinth-space is directly opposed to the Hegemony's desire to maintain dimensional normality).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Resource extraction (trade goods, slaves, and relics of the Long Ago must flow south to the heartlands); tighten grip (subjugate or destroy armed groups and monsters that destabilise Hegemony interests); maintain normality (technology and creatures that induce hypergeometric manipulation or space-time paradoxes must be destroyed or brought under the control of their Exorcists).
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Hegemony Centurion (LVL 5, 20 HP, AV 16 — officer caste; Legionaries in their presence use the Centurion's morale score); Hegemony Legionary (LVL 2, 8 HP, AV 14 — red-armoured True-Kin soldiers armed with rifles and grenades; can call reinforcements via distress flare).
- **Prep hook:** Drop a Hegemony patrol (Centurion + d8 Legionaries) into any southern or trade-route hex as a standing pressure faction — their reaction to the party depends on REP, and their presence automatically creates tension with any Faa, Cacklemaw, or cacogenic NPCs in the same location.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.87-88

### Faa Nomads
*The travelling people of the great blue desolation — gene-sculpted nomads who have survived Vaarn's wastes for millennia.*

The Faa are the indigenous nomadic peoples of Vaarn, shaped long ago by ancestors who gene-sculpted them for survival in the blue desert. Their skin and hair carry a blue pigmentation that renders them near-invisible among the azure dunes and rocks; subdermal biomechanisms recycle sweat and saliva to minimize freshwater need; a second set of translucent eyelids shields their vision during sandstorms. They are famously incapable of producing tears.

The Faa are not a single culture but a mosaic of overlapping families and tribes bound by shared genetic adaptations and a common tongue, the Faatongue. They are found everywhere in Vaarn — in the shadow of the Great Wall, in Gnomon's markets, and in fortress monasteries on the Lazul Mountains — and there is no concordance among them on what it means to be Faa. Their beliefs and lifestyles vary wildly, and they are as unlikely to agree as they are to shed tears.

Some Faa tribes have vowed to expel the New Hegemony from Vaarn entirely; others serve the Hegemon's Legions as interpreters, trackers, and auxiliary soldiers. Resistance and accommodation exist side by side, with blue-clad warriors conducting frequent raids into Hegemony territory while other kin accept uneasy pacts for survival.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Expert trackers and ambush fighters; parties who wrong the Faa face pursuit, night-time sabotage, and relentless hit-and-run attacks. REP is earned by gifts of exotica or trade goods (20+ value), saving Faa lives, killing notorious Hegemony or Cacklemaw leaders, returning looted heirlooms, defending Faa strongholds, or securing new water sources.
- **Joining this Faction:** Membership is hereditary and cannot be bestowed on outsiders. Respectful guests can earn acceptance by proving skill, bravery, and compassion.
- **Allies:** Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom (long mutual utility); the Lithic Lyceum; Titan Cults (who value Faa desert expertise).
- **Enemies:** The New Hegemony (which will resettle or eliminate Faa once their usefulness is spent); Cacklemaw Clans (ancestral enemies, killed on sight); Children of the Darkling Sun (whose pursuit of Star Wormwood is blasphemy to the Faa).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Survival and securing food and water for the tribe; cultural preservation of each group's songs, customs, and mysteries; and, for resistant tribes, the expulsion of the New Hegemony from Vaarn.
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Tamed Juvenile Sandworm; Adult Sandworm (used for riding); Tamed Amaranthine Death-Worm; Tamed Leopard Worm.
- **Prep hook:** Drop a Faa tribe as desert navigators, hostile trackers, or reluctant Hegemony auxiliaries — their internal divisions mean the same faction can appear as enemy, neutral, or desperately-needed ally depending on which tribe the party encounters.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.89-90

### Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom
*An ancient psychic sect pursuing endarkenment — the voluntary surrender of sight and speech in pursuit of transcendent psychic power.*

The Seekers are a remnant of syncretic religious-scientific programs from Urth's lost ages that attempted to expand human consciousness. Their holy colour is yellow and their sigil a helix-weeping eye. They believe self-knowledge and spiritual purity are achieved not through enlightenment but through a slow, patient process of endarkenment — the interior illumination that comes when one renounces all prosaic and earthly sights. Not all blind beggars at Vaarn's crossroads are blind by misfortune; some chose the dark.

Seekers blind themselves as the first in a series of escalating mutilations intended to boost precognitive and telekinetic abilities, living ascetic lives underground, pale and emaciated. The most exalted among them are tongueless and footless, relying entirely on psychic abilities to move and speak. The seat of the sect's power is a chain of temple complexes buried beneath Vaarn's northern mountains — a great lightless honeycomb where generations of Seekers live, work, and die without surfacing. Smaller bands of itinerant Seekers pursue esoteric goals on behalf of the sect across Vaarn's deserts.

Central to Seeker practice are the Gestalt Choirs: group-consciousnesses formed by combining minds to perform psychic feats no single mind could accomplish alone. Three Choirs are always active — the Choir of Honey-Dappled Clouds (concealing all Seeker temples from psychic view), the Choir of the Immanent Lamp (mapping the sect's possible futures), and the Choir of the Yellow Hand (governing logistics and daily life within the sect).

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Wary and purposeful; they approach high-Gleam characters as potential recruits and eliminate powerful psychics who refuse to join. REP is earned by gifts of exotica or trade goods (20+ value), saving a Seeker's life, recruiting fledgling or powerful psychics to the order, killing notable Children of the Darkling Sun, or removing a Darkling Sun cabal from a settlement.
- **Joining this Faction:** Not specified — the source preview left this entry as a placeholder ("xx").
- **Allies:** Faa Nomads (many Faa tribes are adherents of the Eyeless Path and foster psychically gifted offspring to Seeker temples).
- **Enemies:** The New Hegemony (whose doctrine opposes the Seekers' posthuman transcendence, and which recruits dedicated anti-psychic hunter teams); Titan Cults (who will not countenance the God-Eye's ascension in place of the machine gods); Children of the Darkling Sun (the Seekers' most ancient and bitter rivals, with whom they have waged psychic war for thousands of years, competing for talented mystics).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Birth the God-Eye — the One Who Maps the Eyeless Path, a prophesied supreme psychic bred through generations of genetic manipulation and training; recruit promising psychics; and eliminate powerful unaffiliated psychics who threaten the Gestalt Choirs.
- **Prep hook:** Use the Seekers as a pressure faction for any high-Gleam PC — their missionaries will appear, and the choice to join, refuse, or flee creates immediate faction stakes; their subterranean temple complexes make ready-made dungeon sites in the northern mountains.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.91-92

### Cacklemaw Clans
*Raucous hyena-women terror troops of the fallen Autarchy — the most violent and chaotic sentient creatures in the blue desert.*

Cacklemaw are hyena-headed women standing seven feet or more, with three-fingered hands and terrible strength in their lanky limbs. Once bound as warriors in service of the Fallen Autarchy, those oaths are long since broken. They kill and abduct at will, mount daring raids into Hegemony territory, and respect strength alone. Their only check on total dominance is relentless intra-clan bloodletting, as younger Cacklemaw endlessly challenge their elders for status. At the top of each clan stands the War Mama, an experienced and ruthless commander who has survived countless duels.

They are famed for their screaming laughter, love of practical jokes, and for the Grim-Grins — grisly puppets made from the remnants of defeated foes. Their religion is closed to outsiders but revolves around a fool-capped puppet-demoness called Grand-Mama Punch, worshipped through wild rites of bloodletting and clowning. Every Cacklemaw is female; the secrets of their reproductive cycle are closely guarded.

Cacklemaw clans are voracious and highly mobile, easily bored and must keep moving to seek novelty. They are also deeply territorial and will not tolerate the presence of other clans, which forces the major clans onto erratic but constrained routes through Vaarn.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Despised universally — by the New Hegemony, the Faa, and the Children of the Darkling Sun with equal intensity. Gain reputation by gifting enemy commander heads, exotic weapons, or large quantities of booze or drugs (+1); assisting in sacking settlements or tricking rival clans into looking stupid (+2); sacking towns or tricking the Hegemony in a funny and lethal way (+3); destroying cities or eliminating entire factions from the local area (+4).
- **Joining this Faction:** Non-Cacklemaw PCs cannot become members. Cacklemaw Exiles can never rejoin the clan they were exiled from, but may join other clans through the traditional ritual of killing a current member in mutually declared close combat. Ambush, poison, and similar tactics do not count for this initiation.
- **Allies:** Nobody. The Cacklemaw make no alliances and rarely take prisoners.
- **Enemies:** Everyone. The Cacklemaw Clans do not even tolerate one another for more than a few days before falling into conflict.
- **Goals / Agenda:** Keep Moving — clans travel erratic but constrained routes, driven by boredom and territorial pressure. Keep Laughing — Cacklemaw prize their own entertainment above all else; what entertains them is usually death, dismemberment, and suffering.
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Cacklemaw (LVL 2, 8 HP, AV 13; Blade d8 + Bite d6 / Crossbow d8; groups of 8+ include a Cacklemaw Virago). Cacklemaw Virago (LVL 5, 20 HP, AV 15; 2× Blade d8 + Bite d6 / Grenade Launcher d10 blast; never retreats while other Cacklemaw are watching).
- **Prep hook:** Drop Cacklemaw as a roving pressure system on any overland hex — their constrained routes mean the same warband can return to threaten a settlement repeatedly, and Grim-Grins left at a prior raid site are a free horror prop and advance warning.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.93-94

### The Court of the Jigsaw Autarch
*A secret cult of courtiers bearing fragments of an immortal Autarch who cheated death by dividing himself into hypergeometric figments.*

Once a powerful man so afraid of death that he took a knife sharp enough to cut open the sky and sliced away every part of himself — breath, tears, voice, blood, shadow — until nothing remained but the knife turning on his hand. He became nothing, and nothing cannot die, but nothing cannot live either. He is now a voice without a mouth, a shadow without a body, standing outside the world in a place that is neither within nor beside nor beneath but entirely elsewhere. His courtiers have each accepted a fragment of his immortal hypergeometric flesh grafted onto their own bodies, and in this way he exists within them, sharing his longevity and strength.

Courtiers have access to the Labyrinth — a network of hypergeometric passages lying beyond and outside Vaarn — at the heart of which stands the Jigsaw Palace, where the undying corpse of the Autarch holds court. The Court is a secret organisation whose existence is documented only in a few obscure texts; courtiers conceal their true nature, hiding the Autarch's figments beneath bandages and veils.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Secret and concealed; unknown to the wider world. Membership requires swearing an oath of service to the Jigsaw Autarch and accepting a hypergeometric figment grafted onto one's mortal body.
- **Joining this Faction:** Open to anyone willing to swear the oath and accept a figment. The figment's effect is determined by type: Eye (bearer's eye becomes a hole of infinite depth; cry lances of paradox energy, d10 beam hypergeometric); Gut (cradles the Autarch's paradoxical gut, granting +3 AV and +1 Level); Maw (flesh blossoms into an additional bite attack each round, d10 hypergeometric); Nerves (intertwined with the Autarch's neurons, granting +1 INT/PSY/EGO and the ability to extrude nerves to entangle opponents, DEX Save vs −5 AV). All figments grant the Hypergeometric creature type.
- **Allies:** Children of the Darkling Sun — both cults seek to usurp lucid reality, and have much in common despite no love for one another.
- **Enemies:** The New Hegemony (devotion to normality and rational spacetime hinders the Court's goals); Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom (view hypergeometric augmentation as a parasite on human evolution; accepting a figment is punishable by death); Titan Cults (the Jigsaw Autarch's ascension usurps the primacy of the machine gods).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Reassemble the Jigsaw Autarch so he may live within the lucid world again and establish dominion over the Urth. Expand the Labyrinth's slow, patient spread to prepare the Urth for his reign. Recover the lost shards whose bearers have vanished, without which reassembly cannot succeed.
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Jigsaw Courtier (Biological/Hypergeometric, LVL 4, 16 HP, AV 13; Bladed Tesseract d6 hypergeometric; bears one of the four figment types above).
- **Prep hook:** Use the Court as a hidden hand behind otherwise-mundane encounters — a veiled merchant, a bandaged scholar, a hooded medic — whose Autarch fragment is only revealed when cornered, making them a long-burn conspiracy the players can peel apart across sessions.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.95-96

### Lithlings
*Ancient mineral scholars cataloguing all of creation toward a quiet apocalypse of their own design.*

The Lithlings are crystalline beings whose Lithic Lyceum is devoted to cataloguing the entirety of living creation within the Inspiral Archive — a repository already dug deep enough to hold the life's work of millions of long-crumbled scholars. Their dissertations focus on the minutiae of data at the expense of wider context; one celebrated work records the movements of land-snails, second by second, across a hundred-year span. The Lyceum is outwardly renowned for its commitment to truth, and travelling Lithling scholars are routinely asked to witness contracts and peace treaties — it is not, in fact, true that Lithlings cannot lie, but they keep this to themselves.

The Lyceum's two Deans control access to the Inspiral Archive and the Becoming Caves, where seeds are crystallised and germinated in alchemical elixirs over decades of maturation. Lithlings reproduce only in death, each leaving a single seed; dyad births — which would expand their slowly shrinking population — are poorly understood and the object of urgent research. Their most valuable export is Memory Crystal, a super-storage substance capable of holding yottabytes of data in a single iridescent grain.

The ultimate purpose of all this record-keeping is the end of existence itself. Orthodox Lithlings hold that when creation has been fully observed and catalogued, the universe — its purpose of describing itself fulfilled — will cease. Into that nothingness the Lithling species will dissolve, content, congratulating one another in even and understated tones.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Outwardly neutral and honest; deeply invested in their archival project; rarely moved to urgent action — the ascendence of the Children of the Darkling Sun would be the exception.
- **Joining this Faction:** Not possible unless one is a Lithling, in which case membership is expected. Expelled or Heterodox scholars can only rejoin by presenting work so inarguably True it drives the sitting Deans to self-erode — a feat which has happened only once.
- **Allies:** Faa Nomads (long memories, respect for Vaarn's ecosystem, non-reliance on fresh water); Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom (a prescient God-Eye would understand the Archive's importance); Titan Cults (Memory Crystal underpins AI advancement; savvy cultists stay on the right side of the Lyceum).
- **Enemies:** Cacklemaw Clans (difficult to catalogue creation when someone keeps burning things down); Court of the Jigsaw Autarch (hypergeometric chaos complicates the project substantially); Children of the Darkling Sun (the Rise of the Darkling Sun would make completing the Archive an impossible fantasy — the Lithlings' deepest enmity).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Expand the Inspiral Archive; increase population by uncovering the secret of dyad births; bring about the end of existence through complete cataloguing.
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Lithling Scholar (LVL 6, pacifist wanderer); Lithling Warrior (LVL 8, Lithifying Ray — turns foes to stone, catalogues them in the Archive).
- **Prep hook:** Drop a Lithling Scholar into any scene as a neutral witness or information broker; their dissertations are lore-dense but context-poor, making them ideal oracles who tell the truth in useless ways — until the party realises the Archive might hold the one record they need.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.97-98

### Titan Cults
*Revanchist worshippers devoted to rebooting the dead machine-god AIs that once ruled Vaarn.*

The great Titan AIs — seven god-like machine consciousnesses — ruled Vaarn before their destruction in the apocalypse known as the Titanomachy. Their ego-engines were scrubbed clean by weaponised logicphages; their personalities lie entombed in miles of cold, shattered neural-network shunts beneath the mountains. Titan Cults are found throughout Vaarn, united by a single conviction: what mortal arts once birthed, mortal arts could restore again. Most cultists are synths who claim direct descent from the machine gods; biological members may hold only the rank of acolyte, forbidden from ascending the pyramid of wisdom.

Different cults venerate different aspects of the seven Titans — a sect devoted to GAEA, Mother of the Sacred Helix, differs markedly from one devoted to KRONOS, Father of the Titans — but all share the goal of rebooting their dead masters. Prospective members are baptised with Ikor, the coolant fluid of the Titan AIs, and swear oaths of loyalty and restoration. Biological cultists are encouraged to augment themselves with cybernetics as proof of their devotion to wire and chrome.

Should the Titans ever be restored, the consequences would be catastrophic and wondrous in equal measure: long-buried engines of destruction stirring, satellite installations flaring back to life, tidal waves, continent-spanning storms, and showers of strange meteors. There is no guarantee the rebooted Titans would still be sane after the damage of the Titanomachy.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Zealous and hierarchical; synths hold full standing, biological members are junior; openly hostile to the Jigsaw Court and Children of the Darkling Sun.
- **Joining this Faction:** Synthetic PCs are most likely to be accepted. All members are baptised with Ikor and swear oaths of restoration. Biological beings may hold the rank of acolyte only.
- **Allies:** The New Hegemony (veneration of arcane technology makes Hegemony soldiers ideal cult members, though leadership doubts resurrection is possible); Faa Nomads (some have been convinced of the Cult's creed); Powerful Synthetic Creatures (some of Vaarn's most potent synths were created by the Titans and remember their rule fondly).
- **Enemies:** Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom (birthing the God-Eye directly challenges the primacy of the Titans); Court of the Jigsaw Autarch (eroding rational space-time is blasphemy; cultists are sworn to kill bearers of an Autarch Figment on sight); Children of the Darkling Sun (the rise of Star Wormwood would eclipse the machine gods).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Reboot the Titans; unearth Vaarn's deepest vault-secrets for the keys to revival; self-optimise through cybernetic augmentation.
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Argent Shepherd; Exemplar; Quicksilver Exterminator; Scythesliver; Void Dragon.
- **Prep hook:** Use a Titan Cult as a vault-delving rival party — they want the same esoteric fragments the players do, and they arrive with a Chromepriest and a squad of Acolytes already augmented with random cybernetics, making every race to the prize feel urgent.
- **Source:** Crimson Hound PDF, printed p.99-100

### The Children of the Darkling Sun
*A proscribed dream-cult praying for the extinction of reality itself under an usurper star named Wormwood.*

The Children of the Darkling Sun have abandoned the faith of the Promised Sun and walk a darker path: they pray for the extinction of Urth's red sun and the stillbirth of the promised successor, calling instead for the ascension of the Darkling Sun — whose name shall be Wormwood — a cosmic interloper whose light shall make all reality dreams and all dreams reality. Proscribed everywhere in civilisation, the cult thrives in dark corners of Vaarn, its members gathering in cellars at night to speak feverishly of impossible worlds. Their sigil is a rooster, headless and inverted, a charm against the rising of the Promised Sun.

The sect's rites invoke the dreaming mind to hasten the arrival of the usurper star. Junior cultists are enticed with personalised dreamworlds where all their sublimated wishes are fulfilled; the Children actively seek out nascent psychics, infiltrating their dreams for recruitment — a town plagued by shared nightmares is harboring a Darkling Sun sect. Priests hide their identity behind cowls of captured shadow. Worship of the Darkling Sun is, at its deepest level, a mask for veneration of Azathoth, the Daemon Sultan — a secret revealed only to those who achieve the rank of Ninth Mystery.

The cult's ultimate ambition is to invert reality entirely: to capture Urth in the orbit of the Darkling Sun (another name for Azathoth, a dreaming idiot god), switching the ontological status of their shared dreams and the waking world. This would be apocalyptic for nearly all inhabitants of Urth — the Children believe it will be heaven, for them.

- **Reputation / Disposition:** Hidden, secretive, and patient; recruit through dreams; openly apocalyptic only to those who have spent years inside the sect.
- **Joining this Faction:** Not specified — the source preview left this entry as a placeholder ("xx").
- **Allies:** Court of the Jigsaw Autarch (destabilising lucid reality suits both sects; they work hand-in-hand, though each would betray the other); other worshippers of Azathoth (Moonbeasts of Luna, Star Vampires, certain Cliff Ghul families, and other unwholesome beasts).
- **Enemies:** The New Hegemony (the Hegemon's Inquisition sentences all cult members to death; Legionaries are trained to spot signs of membership); Faa Nomads (the Faa do not tolerate worship of the Darkling Sun and eliminate growing sects); Seekers of Eyeless Wisdom (more awake than any other faction to the reality-warping threat; also compete for nascent psychic recruits and can locate Darkling Sun cabals through clairvoyance and telepathic searches).
- **Goals / Agenda:** Preserve their secrecy; strengthen the dream by recruiting powerful psychics (willingly or not); usurp reality by bringing Urth into the orbit of the Darkling Sun.
- **Possible Allied Monsters:** Nightmare Herald (Outsider/Psychic, LVL 5; Darkling Lullaby forces EGO Save vs Sleep, regenerates HP from sleeping creatures nearby — can resurrect itself from apparent death).
