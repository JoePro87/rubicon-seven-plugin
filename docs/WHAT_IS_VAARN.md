# What Is Vaarn?

A one-page orientation for new players. *Vaults of Vaarn* is a science-fantasy
tabletop roleplaying setting created by **Leo Hunt**. This document paraphrases a
handful of high-level facts so you know what world you're stepping into — it is an
original-wording primer, not an excerpt from the rulebook.

## The world in one breath

Vaarn is a dying-earth setting: our own planet, here called **Urth**, unimaginable
ages after a catastrophe called the **Great Collapse** brought the age of the
ancients to an end. What remains is the **Sea of Vaarn** — not water, but an endless
ocean of blue sand. Azure dunes stretch to a horizon that shimmers like a molten
membrane rather than a clean line. They drift back to bare ruins that have lain
untouched for aeons, then bury them again before anyone can name them. It is a place
"deep with secrets as the ocean and twice as capricious."

The high-tech wonders of the old world are still scattered across the waste, but
the knowledge to build or even understand them is mostly gone. Strange, powerful
artifacts called **exotica** are unique, priceless, and never traded — only taken.
There is no money in Vaarn; everything runs on barter. Reality itself is thin in
places: there are psychic souls, hypergeometry, fungal minds, and the radioactive
grave of slain god-titans far to the north.

## The tone and the stories it tells

Vaarn is **strange, beautiful, and dangerous** all at once. It's a sandbox of
exploration and survival, not a plotted story — you wander the blue waste, poke at
ruins, meet factions with their own agendas, and decide what to chase. The world is
meant to feel alive (people want things), hazardous (a fight or a deal can go badly),
and uncanny (almost nothing is quite what you expect). Expect awe and dread in equal
measure: salvage and wonder pulled out of a world that mostly wants to kill you.

A few flavors of the setting worth knowing:

- **Regions** range from the trackless central dune sea (sandworms, Faa nomads) to
  the densely populated southern badlands, floating sky-islands in the east, a
  drowned western coast, a 600-foot broken wall that was once a titan's spine, and
  the poisoned far-northern grave of the titans where time itself frays.
- **Souls** are literal: four immortal souls (alabaster, blue, red, golden) govern
  flesh, fate, memory, and morality — and can be read, harvested, or trapped.
- **Languages** are many and weird, from the synths' mathematical glyph-tongue to a
  psychic speech in which lying is nearly impossible.

## Who you can play (the ancestries)

Character ancestry is rolled or chosen from ten options. As the engine defines them:

1. **True-kin** — unmutated humans who sealed themselves in arcologies through the
   Collapse; caste-bound, tech-hoarding, proud of their pure bloodlines.
2. **Cacogen** — mutated descendants of those left on the surface; warped into a
   million forms, each strange and gifted, and they outnumber the true-kin.
3. **Synth** — thinking machines, once built to serve, now running free in countless
   breeds; they need neither food nor breath.
4. **Newbeast** — uplifted animals who carry their beast nature into how they act
   and survive.
5. **Neobloom** — plant-people who photosynthesize in sunlight, root to heal, and
   are dangerously flammable.
6. **Mycomorph** — fungal beings grown from a human corpse, carrying fragments of
   the dead mind's memories.
7. **Faa nomad** — blue-skinned desert wanderers and sandworm-riders, built to
   endure long stretches without water.
8. **Cacklemaw exile** — outcasts of a ferocious matriarchal people; they struggle
   to show mercy or back down from a fight.
9. **Planeyfolk** — two-dimensional beings like living paintings, who slip through
   cracks and vanish when seen edge-on.
10. **Lithling** — beings of living crystal, immune to many harms, who need neither
    food nor drink.

## How you'll actually play it here

This project, **Rubicon Seven**, runs Vaarn as a **solo experience**: you are the
single player, and **Claude acts as your Dungeon Master**. The AI narrates the world,
plays every NPC, calls for rolls, and tracks the mechanics behind the scenes through
a custom tool engine — so you never have to manage turns, encounter checks, or
bookkeeping. You describe what your character does; the DM handles the rest.
